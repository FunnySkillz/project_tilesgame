# Manual-First Progression Pivot

## Decision

Coldwater Catch is not primarily a tile-placement strategy game. It is a tactile mobile progression game in which the player begins as one fisherman, experiences each job directly, then earns people and machines that take over those jobs.

The production fantasy is:

Manual work -> money -> upgrade -> visible power increase -> automation -> larger operation.

## Core Rule

Never automate a job before the player has performed and understood it manually.

This gives every worker, machine, and vehicle a clear emotional payoff. The player should think: "I used to carry every one of those fish. Now my fishery does it."

## First Playable Loop

1. Tap Walk and move the fisherman to the dock.
2. Tap Fish on a nearby visible fish to fill the basket.
3. Walk to the pool to drop fish, or to the cutter to process it.
4. Stand at the cutter while it turns fish into meat.
5. Walk meat to a waiting buyer at the market.
6. Spend the money on a larger net.

The first minute should feel almost deliberately simple. It creates the vocabulary for every later system.

## Automation Ladder

1. Net upgrade: basket/net capacity becomes visibly larger.
2. First fisherman: repeats shore fishing and pool delivery.
3. Cutter worker: keeps a familiar cutter busy.
4. Market worker: serves the buyer queue.
5. First area unlock: reveals the next named work district.
6. Boat: takes the player offshore to catch schools and silverfish.
7. Storage, smoker, freezer: turn greater volume into better goods.
8. Truck orders: turn prepared stock into satisfying bulk sales.
9. Deep water: add cold, rare fish, and later predators.

## Spatial Rules

The 16x18 map and camera remain useful. They give the operation room to visibly grow.

The target expansion model is area unlocks, not individual optimisation purchases. Each purchased area should have a clear name, price, visual reveal, and purpose:

- Processing Yard: storage and smoker.
- Second Dock: boat berth and larger net.
- Cold Store: freezer and preserved goods.
- Loading Yard: truck trade.
- Deep-Water Pier: rare fish and danger.

Tiles can still support placement and readable walkable surfaces, but adjacency bonuses must remain optional polish rather than the source of early-game success.

## Deferred Systems

Cold waves, attacks, fences, traps, weapons, repairs, and monster resources remain part of the long-term game. They begin only once catch-carry-process-sell and the first automations are enjoyable on their own.

## Current Foundation

Implemented in Phase 4.7:

- controllable visible fisherman
- manual shore fishing with a three-fish basket
- visible fish and meat carried by the player
- manual cutter and hand sale loop
- no starter worker
- workers act as automation switches when later hired
- Net 2 unlocks the boat rather than showing it from the first second

Next implementation focus: physical worker routes, world-visible upgrade states, and named area unlocks.
