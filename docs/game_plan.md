# Coldwater Catch

Coldwater Catch is a portrait-first fishing-business progression game about one person building a rough fishery on the edge of dangerous cold water. The player begins on foot with a small basket, catches visible fish, carries them through processing, and personally sells meat to buyers before hiring people and unlocking machines to automate each familiar job.

The prototype world is larger than one phone screen. A 16x18 territory contains a compact starter fishery, then a broad locked frontier that the player claims outward tile by tile. The player pans the camera to work across the growing settlement while keeping individual tiles legible.

## Core Loop

1. Walk the fisherman to the shore and catch visible fish by hand.
2. Carry fish to a pool or cutter.
3. Process fish into meat while standing at the cutter.
4. Carry meat to a waiting buyer and take payment directly.
5. Spend money on upgrades, workers, named expansion areas, machines, and eventually boats.

## Prototype Scope

The first playable version focuses only on the loop:

- water tiles spawn visible swimming fish
- the player is a visible, tap-to-move fisherman with fish and meat shown above their head
- manual shore fishing fills a small basket before the boat is unlocked
- the player deposits fish, operates the cutter, and hands meat to buyers in the first loop
- Net 2 unlocks the boat as the first offshore fishing upgrade
- shallow, open, cold, deep, and monster water create different fishing routes
- harder fishing routes give small discovery bonuses and warnings before danger escalates
- land, dock, and plaza tiles accept buildings
- pools increase live fish capacity
- cutters turn live fish into meat
- markets sell meat to waiting buyers
- buyers walk to markets, wait, buy, and leave
- villagers, cooks, and merchants ask for different goods
- visible buyer wants make meat, smoked meat, and bulk selling distinct
- dock orders create larger timed selling moments
- dock and plaza surfaces make the base read like a working waterfront
- workers are a later automation reward; they repeat work the player has already performed
- storage increases processed-goods capacity
- the current frontier provides space, while the target design unlocks named usable areas rather than asking players to buy scattered single tiles
- Map mode pans across the larger fishery; Center returns to the starter district
- smokers turn meat into higher-value smoked meat
- upgrades improve net, boat speed/hold, pools, and cutters

Later systems should layer onto the same grid:

- cold and heaters
- monsters and defenses
- larger fish and stronger machines
- spoilage and freezers
- buyer types, preferences, and larger trade orders
- roads, trucks, and bulk orders
- extra resources such as wood, scrap, fuel, oil, bones, and monster parts
